# Deriving the plan from narration, video and photos

The plan is reasoned out, not computed. Work in this order; each step narrows the next.

## 1. Skeleton from the video

- Start at the front door. Note the timestamp at which each room is entered and what the narration says there.
- Write the walk as a list: `00:01 front door → 00:04 guest toilet (left) → 00:10 living room (ahead) → …`.
  These become the blue numbered `steps` on the draft so the owner can follow your reading.
- For each transition note the turn (left / right / straight) and whether a door or a plain opening was passed.
- Decide the plan's orientation once: e.g. "window wall of the living room is the top edge". Keep it.

## 2. Rooms as rectangles, then refine

- Put each room down as a rectangle in walk order, respecting left/right. Shared walls first: two rooms either side of
  a corridor share its walls; a guest toilet beside the entrance shares the entrance wall.
- Size: count floor tiles along each wall in the photos (find the tile size from a door: an interior door leaf is
  about 0.8 m wide and 2.05 m high). Radiators are usually 1.0–1.4 m long, kitchen base units 0.6 m deep, upper
  units 0.35 m, a fridge niche 0.65–0.9 m wide. Ceiling height 2.7–2.9 m unless seen otherwise.
- Check closure: rooms around a corridor must add up along both of its sides. A gap or overlap means a wrong size or
  a missing recess, light well or duct.

## 3. Wall by wall

For every wall of every room answer from a photo: where does it start and end, what is on it (door, window, radiator,
units), and does it step in or out anywhere. Use the photo numbers: neighbours in the numbering were taken from nearby.

A wall is **known** only if a photo shows it at an angle with both corners. A wall seen end-on, or seen only from far
away along its length, is **assumed** — recesses, piers and chamfers hide there. Typical traps:

- an open kitchen that looks like it is "on the back wall" but sits in a recess beside a neighbouring room;
- a pier or column in a corner (structural), often beside a balcony door;
- a chamfered corner at the mouth of a corridor;
- a bedroom entered through a short vestibule beside a built-in wardrobe;
- windows onto a light well (frosted, no view) versus windows onto the street;
- a door that is in the side wall of a room, not the wall you first assumed.

## 4. Handedness check

Pick two moments in the video. For each, name what is on the camera's left and right and straight ahead, then check
the plan gives the same answer from that position and heading. A mirrored plan passes every other test.

## 5. Questions and draft

Everything assumed goes into `questions` with its segment; they are drawn red on the draft and asked in chat:

> ۱. برش مورب بین راهرو و ورودی را حدود ۱٫۴ متر گرفته‌ام؛ درست است؟
> ۲. دیوار پشت کابینت‌ها: آشپزخانه هم‌خط دیوار سرویس است یا عقب‌تر نشسته؟

Also ask about any conflict between narration and images, and for one real measurement.

Send the draft PNG. Say which parts rest on narration, which on video, which on photos. Wait for confirmation.
When the owner marks a correction, re-derive the neighbouring walls too: a recess moves the fixtures, the area and
often a door.
