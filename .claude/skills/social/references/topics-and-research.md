# Topics and research

## 1. The north star: the viewer's own life, told so they share it
Evidence from a page where almost every follower came from three reels out of ~40: the winners (311k, 116k,
59k views; 39k / 9.5k / 10.7k shares) all spoke about **the viewer's own job, money, safety, family or daily
routine**, in a way people **send to someone they care about**. Every other reel sat at 1.5–2.5k views.

- **Topic test** (record it in the concept): whose money, job, safety, health, family or routine changes, and
  what should they do about it? Mark `life: true/false` and `life_impact`. At least 2 of every 3 reels are
  `life: true`; a gate blocks a third non-life reel in a row.
- **Share value:** would a viewer send this to family or a friend? Every reel and carousel ends with one
  share ask that fits the topic ("send this to your mum", "send it to the friend who…") and gives one concrete
  thing to do or keep (a checklist, a step, a number, a prompt).
- **Connect the page's two halves.** A niche page (e.g. "AI in daily life") must tie every topic to both
  halves: life AND the niche. Purely social or purely technical topics are out unless the owner asks.
- **Audience reality check:** every tip and number is checked against the audience's real situation (local
  currency and prices, which services and payments work for them, internet limits, law). A tip that needs a
  blocked service or an unaffordable product is useless: say so or give what works for them.
- **Tone:** calm, practical, true. A frightening subject is told as "how to be ready", with a reputable
  source; no predictions of war or crashes, no fake numbers.

## 2. Topic filter (from an owner's review of a 72-topic bank)
- **News alone is not a topic.** Each topic needs a use the viewer can act on, or a surprising view of their
  own life through the niche.
- **Statistics and economy in the niche's voice:** "officials said inflation is X; when I (the AI) looked
  closer I got a different result…" — the "different result" is a real calculation from sourced data, shown
  step by step, never an invented number or a conspiracy.
- **Out:** social warnings and policing ("don't rent out your bank card"), price news with no niche angle.
- **In:** one concrete task the viewer can do today with the niche's tools, step by step ("hand your
  monthly spending to an AI").
- **Never an anti-ad:** a topic that casts doubt on the very category the page's own product sells.

## 3. Weekly topic bank with owner approval
Once a week a research team (parallel subagents: trend radar, local news and economy, jobs, family and
education, money and income, tech-in-life) writes **≥ 3 topics per slot of the next week** to a sheet tab:
week, id, headline (angle H/W/N), one-paragraph explanation, why the audience cares, life impact, series
potential, sources, suggested format, empty `Approved` checkbox. The owner ticks; each prepare takes the next
ticked, unused topic in sheet order and marks it `Used`; with none ticked it falls back to live research.
When the owner says "only my ticked list, in order", that is the only source.

## 4. Live research rules
- Window: last 7 days; widen to 30 days only when nothing fits (log it). Never repeat a story covered in the
  last 14 posts/reels (same event, product, study or organisation = same story, whatever the headline).
- Open the **primary source** (paper, regulator, company newsroom, official report). If the network blocks it,
  fetch it through a cloud drive's URL import; only if that fails too, confirm in ≥ 3 independent reputable
  outlets and drop any figure they disagree on.
- Record: publish date, event date, country, organisation, exact claim, limits, link. An old study re-reported
  today is not new.
- Medical: label the evidence level; never treatment or diet advice; add "this is not medical advice".
- Company claims are attributed to the company. Drop rumours, denied stories, conclusions beyond the evidence.
- Dossier fields: summary, source, confirming[], **allowed_claims[]** (every name, number and quote on screen
  must be in it), limits[], why it matters, image rights, dedupe key, candidates considered with drop reasons.
- **Practical advice needs a research dossier** with ≥ 3 reputable sources (Red Cross/Red Crescent, WHO,
  official bodies, the service's own docs), each tip checked to work for this audience, and what NOT to do. A
  tip without a source is dropped.

## 5. Field rotation and cooldown
- Never two posts in a row from the same field; no field more than twice in 7 days; prefer the least recent.
- A reel's field differs from the last 4 reels and the last 2 days of posts.
- Keep 1 in 4 as an exploration pick (an untried angle, field or format).
- Job/life-group call-outs: at least one a day naming a group ("if you cook…", "hairdressers, get ready…");
  never the same group twice in 7 days; speak to their goal, never shame a body, class or group.

## 6. Viral radar: learn from winners, never repost them
- Daily: list the last 7 days of reels from the niche's peer pages (a scraper API; ~1 credit per page) plus a
  keyword search; rank by plays and by plays ÷ the page's usual plays (an outlier beats a big page's average).
  Save the top 10 with hook line, format and CTA trick.
- At least one reel a day can be **our own remake** of a radar winner: same topic and angle, our headline,
  voice, motion and newer facts; never their footage, audio or script. Copy techniques freely (hook structure,
  keyword-comment CTA, debate question); credit the idea ("idea from @page") when it is clearly theirs.
- **Never download and repost another page's video, even with "presented by @page" in the caption**, unless
  the owner of that video gave written permission. Credit is not permission: it is still their copyright, a
  report can remove it and strike the account, and platforms rank reposts lower. With permission, prefer a
  Collab post; log the permission. Offer the owner a ready permission message instead.

## 7. Real people
- Famous, trending people carry reach: when the story is really about one, the cover and frame 0 show their
  **real photo** (Wikimedia Commons, official press photos), credited in the caption.
- Never a generated or altered likeness of a real person, never a fake or sharpened quote.
- Generated scenes must not contain real people either: image models add portraits of politicians to
  classroom walls and faces to banknotes. Check every panel and drop it if any appear.
